25 August 2026 · for cofounder share · not a public page

These seven are not one category. Norm Ai is the only one that reviews marketing language. Credo and Trustible govern a company’s own AI systems. Vanta, Drata, and Sprinto sell SOC 2 / ISO evidence. OneTrust is privacy plus enterprise GRC with an AI module bolted on.

Under Intelligence is none of those. It is paid-media claim review: trigger, then gate, then a clause on the graph. Steal their clarity (named buyer, workflow, evidence). Do not steal their category words.

01Norm Ai

Highest overlap

norm.ai · “Agentic Law”

Who they sell to

Legal and compliance at large financial institutions (asset managers, broker-dealers, insurers). Clients and backers include Blackstone, Vanguard, Citi, New York Life, TIAA. Combined AUM they cite is ~$30–35T. Buyers are GC / CCO / legal-ops, not trafficking.

What they do

“Agentic law”: encode regs into AI agents. Closest product to us is regulated content review — marketing collateral, investor decks, disclosures, web/social — against SEC, FINRA, FTC, NAIC, FCA, MiFID II, plus firm policy. Also DDQ/RFP, contract review, disclosure libraries, fact banks. Review is LLM agents with citations and suggested rewrites, plus human oversight. Not a lexical matcher.

Shared with us

Same job on a different stack: take copy, flag risk, cite a rule, keep a decision record, keep a human in the loop. Shared nouns: citations, disclosures, substantiation, review cycle time, marketing vs compliance.

What to change on the site

This is the only company that should shape the homepage.

  • Name the buyer the way they do: “marketing compliance at a regulated advertiser,” not “trafficking & creative.”
  • Add a coverage map like theirs (SEC / FINRA / FTC / Green Guides / platform policies) so we do not look like a toy next to “100+ global regulations.”
  • Keep the comparison table, but add a row they cannot match: same text → same pack hits. Their agents will not say that.
  • Do not copy “agentic law,” affiliated law firm, or “Legal AGI.” That is their brand, not ours.

02Credo AI

Thin overlap

credo.ai · AI governance platform

Who they sell to

Fortune 500 / Global 2000 AI governance: Mastercard, insurers, healthcare, federal. Buyers are CAIO, CDO, CRO, GC. Forrester Leader in AI governance. Problem they sell: “you cannot govern millisecond agents with a monthly audit.”

What they do

Registry of every AI system, agent, model, and vendor. Policy packs for EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, ISO 42001. Risk scoring, shadow-AI discovery, audit evidence, 300+ integrations (Snowflake, Databricks, ServiceNow). They govern whether you may deploy an AI system, not whether an ad claim is substantiated.

Shared with us

Thin: “policy packs,” audit evidence, human review, mapping controls to a named standard. Their packs are GRC checklists. Ours are gated lexical patterns over ad copy.

What to change on the site

  • Drop any leftover AI-governance smell. “Compliance layer” is fine; “govern AI” is their category and we lose it.
  • If we keep “policy packs,” say ad-claim packs (Green Guides, Made in USA, health) so nobody hears Credo.
  • Do not add EU AI Act / ISO 42001 chips. Those buyers will bounce when they see Meta / Google / TikTok.

03Trustible

Thin overlap

trustible.ai · AI governance for the governance office

Who they sell to

Enterprise AI governance teams (legal, risk, AI strategy), not engineers. Customers they name: Leidos, Nuix, Ashoka. Smaller than Credo (~$4.6M seed). Sell a 30/60/90-day program: inventory → risk workflows → board reporting.

What they do

Intake of every AI use case / model / vendor. Risk triage (low-risk auto-approves). Map once to EU AI Act / NIST / ISO 42001. Spreadsheet replacement for the AI governance office.

Shared with us

Intake → risk route → audit trail. That is the shape of ReviewLens, not the object. They route an AI use-case. We route a creative.

What to change on the site

  • Steal the lifecycle sentence, rewritten for ads: ingest → trigger → gate → human record. We already have this; make it the first thing a CCO sees.
  • Steal “low-risk moves fast, high-risk gets a person.” Map it to: licensed hits clear; absolute packs and borderline go to ReviewLens.
  • Do not add “AI inventory,” “vendor questionnaire,” or “framework readiness %.” Wrong product.

04Vanta

UX / sales only

vanta.com · Agentic Trust Platform (SOC 2)

Who they sell to

Startups first (need SOC 2 to close deals), then mid-market security, then CISO. 16,000+ customers. Buyer is Head of Security / founder, not brand or media.

What they do

Continuous compliance automation: SOC 2, ISO 27001, HIPAA, GDPR, plus ISO 42001 / NIST AI RMF as add-ons. Integrations collect evidence. Trust Center + questionnaire bot. “Agentic Trust Platform.”

Shared with us

Almost none on the matcher. Shared sales motion: compliance as something you show a counterparty so a deal unblocks. Their artifact is a SOC 2 report. Ours would be a cited verdict on a creative.

What to change on the site

  • Steal “unblock the cycle.” Their quote is questionnaires blocking sales. Ours is legal blocking launch. CTA should be “get this live before the next flight,” not “talk to a governance expert.”
  • Steal Trust Center energy only if we mean a sample audit graph, not a security portal.
  • Do not use “trust platform,” “get compliant,” or SOC 2 / HIPAA chips. That is how we get filed next to Vanta and ignored.

05Drata

UX / sales only

drata.com · continuous GRC for security teams

Who they sell to

Security-led teams from growth-stage to enterprise (~8,500 customers). CISO / GRC lead who already has a program and wants deeper control monitoring than Vanta.

What they do

Continuous control testing across 30+ frameworks, 300+ integrations, auditor workspace, Trust Center. Newer pitch: discover AI agents in the environment and prove policy before they act. Still your security posture, not the ad’s claims.

Shared with us

Continuous monitoring + evidence that stays current. Analog: we could monitor live ads, not cloud configs. Also: map a control once, reuse it. That is already our pack model.

What to change on the site

  • One line for always-on: “same packs on every impression, not a quarterly legal review.”
  • Do not promise “discover every AI agent.” That is their wedge; we do not have it and should not pretend.

06OneTrust

Adjacent (consent, not claims)

onetrust.com · privacy + GRC + AI module

Who they sell to

Fortune 500 privacy, legal, and data-protection offices. Cookie banners, DSAR, consent. Half of Fortune 500. CMOs care about the consent module (IAB TCF, Google Consent Mode), not the AI-governance module.

What they do

Privacy automation, consent/preferences, third-party risk, tech risk, plus an AI use-case workflow (intake → assess → approve → monitor). Adjacent to ads only on whether you may process the user’s data, not whether the claim is lawful.

Shared with us

Marketing-adjacent compliance; named regulations; audit trail. Different object: personal data / cookies vs substantiation of a claim.

What to change on the site

  • Draw a hard line: we do claim legality, not cookie consent. One sentence so a DPO does not think we replace OneTrust.
  • Do not add a cookie-banner / preference-center story. Wrong war.
  • If we ever name OneTrust, it is as a complement (“they capture consent; we cite the claim”).

07Sprinto

UX / first-run only

sprinto.com · Autonomous Trust Platform

Who they sell to

Series A–C companies that need a SOC 2 operator because they have no compliance hire. 3,000+ customers. More prescriptive and cheaper than Vanta/Drata. Also selling “autonomous trust” and an AI-tool registry.

What they do

Connect AWS/GitHub/HR, collect evidence, map 200+ frameworks, vendor diligence, Trust Center. AI governance here = shadow-AI registry mapped to ISO 42001 — still not ad review.

Shared with us

Guided first-run for a team with no specialist. That is PolicyLens: paste copy, get a cited hit, no GRC implementation.

What to change on the site

  • Steal time-to-first-value: “no 90-day implementation.” We already say ready for a trafficking team; make the contrast explicit vs GRC installs.
  • Keep the self-serve CTA. Do not add “request a demo / talk to a named advisor” as the primary path unless we are selling enterprise Norm-style.

Website implicationWhat this means for underintelligence.com

Keep the current spine: Ad claims, handled. Deterministic matcher. PolicyLens / ReviewLens / API. Trigger-then-gate. Citation table vs LLM-only.

Add (from this list)

  1. Named buyer — brand legal / marketing compliance / trafficking, not “governance teams.”
  2. What we are not — one line: not SOC 2, not EU AI Act inventory, not cookie consent.
  3. Coverage as chips that match the buyer — FTC, FDA, NAD, Green Guides, Made in USA, Meta/Google/TikTok stay; never add SOC 2 / ISO 27001 / ISO 42001.
  4. Workflow speed — low-risk licensed copy clears; absolute and borderline go to ReviewLens.
  5. Complement, don’t collide — OneTrust/Vanta are the customer’s other stack; we sit on the creative.

Do not add

Agentic trust, AI registry, vendor questionnaires, framework readiness percentages, “govern every agent,” affiliated law firm.

Closest real competitor on this list: Norm Ai, and only on FINRA/SEC-style marketing review. Differentiation is already on the page and should stay loud: the model does not decide the hit. Their agents do.
